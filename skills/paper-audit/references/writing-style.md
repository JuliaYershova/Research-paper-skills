# Writing Style

Used by paper-audit (check J) when reviewing text, and by add-results (Step 7) when drafting.

## Rules

A results text reports what was done and what was found. Every sentence must carry information a reader needs.

Cut or rewrite:
- **Filler.** "It is worth noting that", "Interestingly", "In this section we", "As can be seen", "It should be emphasised".
- **Defensive writing.** Pre-empting reviewers ("While some may argue", "We do not claim"), apologies for limitations outside the limitations section, and repeated justification of a choice already explained.
- **Inflated wording.** "significantly" without a statistical test, "substantially", "remarkably", "crucially", "fully", "seamless", "perfectly", "state-of-the-art" without a comparison that shows it.
- **Stacked hedges.** "may potentially suggest", "could possibly indicate". One hedge, or none if the evidence is clear.
- **Repetition.** The same number or claim stated in the text and again in the next paragraph, or a caption retold in the text.
- **Interpretation beyond the data.** Explanations of why something happened that no result or analysis in the paper supports. Either point to the evidence or mark it as a hypothesis in one short clause.

Keep:
- The numbers, with the table or figure reference.
- Negative and mixed results, stated as plainly as positive ones.
- One sentence of interpretation per finding, when the evidence supports it.

## Co-authors' text

Text in the paper was written by co-authors. Review it carefully and edit it minimally:
- Change only what the evidence requires (a wrong number, a claim the results no longer support) or what the user asks for.
- Keep their wording, structure and voice. Do not restyle a paragraph to fix one sentence.
- Style suggestions on existing text are listed as suggestions, never applied unless the user approves each one.

## Length

When a page or word limit applies (see [venue-requirements.md](venue-requirements.md)), state the current length, the limit, and how much the proposed changes add. If the result is over the limit, propose specific cuts, starting with filler and repetition, then moving detail to the appendix. Never cut results or limitations to make space without asking.
